About StatLab

StatLab is an interactive, visual introduction to probability and statistics for undergraduates of any major. Every core idea is taught through something you can manipulate. Drag a point, draw a sample, move a slider, and watch the mathematics respond. Text explains, and interaction convinces.

Who

Made by Dr. Sein Minn and Kaung Hein Htet at the Asian Institute of Technology (AIT). The site is built for teaching and is free for anyone to use in a course, a lecture, or on their own.

What it follows

Core Concepts (Chapters 1–13) follows OpenStax Introductory Statistics by Barbara Illowsky and Susan Dean chapter for chapter, so each section can be read in the free book and then explored here. Advanced Concepts (Chapters 14–17) adds counting, Bayesian inference, resampling, and multivariable thinking, and each one branches off the core chapter it depends on. The landing page shows that order as a path.

Resources & Credits

StatLab stands on work other people gave away, and the debts are worth naming.

  • OpenStax Introductory Statistics by Barbara Illowsky and Susan Dean, licensed CC BY 4.0 and free at openstax.org. Chapters 1–13 follow its structure, and the explanatory text here is written to sit alongside it.
  • Seeing Theory (Daniel Kunin and colleagues, Brown University) for structural inspiration only. It showed that a statistics course can be a sequence of things you manipulate. No text, code, or design was copied from it.
  • D3 v7 by Mike Bostock and contributors (ISC License), which draws every chart on the site. It is the only vendored library.
  • KaTeX (MIT License) typesets the formulas. It is the site's only external request, and the pages stay usable without it.

Per-chapter reading references appear on the chapter pages themselves, at the end of each section, naming the exact book section that section corresponds to.

Privacy

The site is static files only. There are no accounts, no cookies, no analytics, and no third-party trackers. Your choice of light or dark theme is the one thing kept, in your own browser, under the key statlab-theme. Nothing is sent anywhere, and blocking storage only means the theme resets on the next page. The only external resource is the KaTeX library for typesetting formulas; if it does not load, formulas appear as plain LaTeX and everything else still works.

Conventions

Quantiles use the Weibull method, position (n + 1)p. Sample variance divides by n − 1. Capital letters denote random variables and lowercase letters their observed values. These choices are stated in a footnote the first time each chapter uses them.
